# Phrase swaps for difficult conversations

Practical wording already used in the free Communication Style Quiz tips and notes. These are starting lines, not scripts you have to follow, and not a claim that one phrase “fixes” a hard conversation.

[Take the free quiz](https://commstylequiz.com/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=a_repo&utm_content=phrase_swaps) if you want a snapshot of which habit you reach for first.

## One question that often helps

Before you solve, plan, or pitch, ask:

> “Do you want a decision, a listen, a plan, or ideas?”

Then match that for a few minutes.

## If this habit is yours

### You often want Clarity (The Pathfinder)

A short request and a clear yes or no help you relax. If a conversation has been circling, you may jump to “so what are we actually doing?” That can sound blunt. It is often an attempt to respect everyone’s time.

| Instead of… | Try… |
| --- | --- |
| Jumping straight to the ask | One beat of context first: “I know this landed suddenly. Here's what I need by Thursday.” |
| A quick answer | Ask one question about how the other person sees it, then answer. |
| Moving on while someone still needs to talk it through | Set a timer in your head and just listen for two minutes. |

Add one sentence of context or appreciation before a direct request — it lands better.

### You often want Connection (The Bridge-Builder)

You may not be able to use a suggestion until you feel the other person has actually heard you. A neat plan offered too early can feel like a brush-off, even when it was meant kindly.

| Instead of… | Try… |
| --- | --- |
| Saving your real view for the end (or keeping it to yourself to keep the peace) | State your own view in the first half of the conversation. |
| A soft maybe that turns into an overloaded week | A kind but clear “no”: “I can't take this on this week.” |
| Warm listening that never turns into a next step | After listening, summarize the decision or next step. |

### You often want Structure (The Blueprint)

You think in steps. Messy brainstorms can wear you out, not because you dislike ideas, but because you cannot tell what is decided.

| Instead of… | Try… |
| --- | --- |
| Starting with all the detail | Lead with the conclusion, then offer the detail: “Short version: yes. Here's why...” |
| Polishing alone until it feels finished | Share a rough draft earlier than feels comfortable — feedback beats polishing alone. |
| Improvising in a fast conversation | “Let me come back to you on that by 3 pm.” |

### You often want Spark (The Spark)

You light up when there is room to play. You can also pile idea on idea until the original question is lost.

| Instead of… | Try… |
| --- | --- |
| Ending a lively burst with no owner | Write down one owner and one date for the best idea. |
| Adding the next idea right away | Pause and invite one reaction before you add another. |
| A long, exciting message where the point is buried | For important messages, cut your draft by a third and keep the single key point. |

### You often switch (The Navigator)

Tell people what you need from a conversation: a decision, a listen, a plan or ideas. Use your range on purpose: pick the style the other person seems to prefer. Notice which style you drop under stress — that's your real default.

## If you are talking to someone who wants…

### Clarity

Lead with the request, then offer detail.

> “Lead with the point. Tell me what you need and by when.”

### Connection

Reflect back one thing you heard before you solve it:

> “I can see why that felt unfair.”

Check in with them as a person first. Tell them how you really feel about it.

### Structure

A three-bullet recap at the end of a call is often worth more than another ten minutes of talk. Give them the details and a little time. Specific examples help more than general feelings.

### Spark

Leave a little space for “what else could we try?” before you lock the plan. Talk it through live if you can. Build on their idea before you poke holes in it.

## A few more lines from the quiz tips

- “So what are we deciding?”
- “Which part, exactly?”
- “I can't take this on this week.”
- “Short version: yes. Here's why...”
- “Let me come back to you on that by 3 pm.”
- “Do you want a decision, a listen, a plan, or ideas?”
