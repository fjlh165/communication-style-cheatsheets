# Four communication styles — at a glance

A short vocabulary for what someone seems to want from a conversation right now: **a decision, a listen, a plan, or a new idea**.

These are everyday habits, not types of person and not medical categories. Nobody is only one of them, and people switch. Someone who wants connection at home may want clarity at work.

If you want a snapshot of which habit showed up most in *your* answers, [take the free 13-question quiz](https://commstylequiz.com/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=a_repo&utm_content=cheatsheet) (about three minutes, no sign-up). Your answers are scored in your browser and are not sent to the site.

## The four habits

| Habit | In one line | What it measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Clarity** | Gets to the point | How quickly you move a conversation toward a clear answer or decision. |
| **Connection** | Leads with empathy | How much you track the people and feelings in a conversation, not only the content. |
| **Structure** | Thinks in steps | How much you prefer order, detail and a plan before you speak or act. |
| **Spark** | Brings energy and ideas | How much you use energy, humour and new angles to move a conversation. |

(“Humour” is the spelling used in the quiz data.)

## The five results

The quiz names a main style from the habit that scored highest. If your highest and lowest scores are within 2 points, the result is **The Navigator** (balanced). If your second style is only one point behind, the result page says so — that is the scoring being honest about a mixed set of answers, not a special category of person.

### The Pathfinder (Clarity)

**Says it straight, gets it done.** Clear, decisive, and focused on what's next.

You value clarity. You say what you mean, move conversations toward decisions and respect other people's time.

**In the wild**

- Your texts are short, and people know exactly what you're asking.
- In a stalled discussion, you're the one who says “So what are we deciding?”
- You'd rather hear the bad news now than a nice version later.

**Watch out for (under stress).** You may get more blunt and skip the “why” — others can hear that as impatience.

**Try this**

1. Before giving a quick answer, ask one question about how the other person sees it.
2. Add one sentence of context or appreciation before a direct request — it lands better.
3. When someone needs to talk something through, set a timer in your head and just listen for two minutes.

**How to talk to a Pathfinder:** Lead with the point. Tell them what you need and by when.

### The Bridge-Builder (Connection)

**Hears what people don't say.** Warm, attentive, and tuned in to people.

You value connection. You notice how others feel, make space for quieter voices and build trust.

**In the wild**

- You notice when someone in the group chat has gone quiet.
- People tell you things they haven't told anyone else.
- You think about how a message will land before you hit send.

**Watch out for (under stress).** You may go along with others to keep the peace and keep your real view to yourself.

**Try this**

1. State your own view in the first half of the conversation, not only at the end.
2. Practice a kind but clear “no”: “I can't take this on this week.”
3. After listening, summarize the decision or next step so the warmth turns into action.

**How to talk to a Bridge-Builder:** Check in with them as a person first. Tell them how you really feel about it.

### The Blueprint (Structure)

**Has a plan for the plan.** Thoughtful, precise, and organized.

You value structure. You think before you speak, bring order to messy discussions and get details right.

**In the wild**

- You're the one who pins the address and the time.
- “Which part, exactly?” is one of your favorite questions.
- You'd rather reply well tomorrow than badly right now.

**Watch out for (under stress).** You may go quiet to think, and others can read the silence as disagreement.

**Try this**

1. Lead with your conclusion, then offer the detail: “Short version: yes. Here's why...”
2. Share a rough draft earlier than feels comfortable — feedback beats polishing alone.
3. In fast conversations, it's fine to say “Let me come back to you on that by 3 pm.”

**How to talk to a Blueprint:** Give them the details and a little time. Specific examples help more than general feelings.

### The Spark (Spark)

**Turns any chat into an idea.** Energetic, imaginative, and persuasive.

You value possibility. You bring energy, see new angles and get people excited to act.

**In the wild**

- You'd rather call than type three long messages.
- You can turn an awkward silence into a running joke.
- Your best ideas show up while you're talking, not before.

**Watch out for (under stress).** You may talk faster and jump between ideas, and the main point can get lost.

**Try this**

1. End brainstorms by writing down one owner and one date for the best idea.
2. Pause after sharing an idea and invite one reaction before adding the next.
3. For important messages, cut your draft by a third and keep the single key point.

**How to talk to a Spark:** Talk it through with them live. Build on their idea before you poke holes in it.

### The Navigator (balanced)

**Speaks every style fluently.** Adaptable — you switch styles to fit the moment.

Your answers spread fairly evenly across all four dimensions. You can be direct, warm, structured or playful depending on the situation.

**In the wild**

- You talk differently to your boss, your best friend and your group chat — on purpose.
- You often end up translating between people who don't get each other.
- People with very different styles all find you easy to talk to.

**Watch out for (under stress).** You may lose your range and fall back on one style — notice which one.

**Try this**

1. Notice which style you drop under stress — that's your real default.
2. Tell people what you need from a conversation: a decision, a listen, a plan or ideas.
3. Use your range on purpose: pick the style the other person seems to prefer.

**How to talk to a Navigator:** Tell them what this conversation is for: a decision, a listen, a plan or ideas.

## Using this without turning it into a label

The useful move is not to typecast a partner or a colleague. It is to ask, in ordinary language: “Do you want a decision, a listen, a plan, or ideas?” Then match that for a few minutes.

Most people talk differently to a boss, a best friend and a group chat. The site describes adjusting your style to the other person as “communication accommodation.” Treat a quiz result as a default, not a fixed label.

The names are just labels for those habits. “Pathfinder” means you often reached for clarity; it does not mean you are a type of person in any official sense.
