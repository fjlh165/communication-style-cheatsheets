# Conflict-repair checklist

A one-page list compiled from the free Communication Style Quiz tips and notes. Print it, or keep it next to a hard conversation. It is a reflection aid, not a therapy protocol, and not a promise that any conversation will go well.

[Take the free quiz](https://commstylequiz.com/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=a_repo&utm_content=conflict_checklist) if you want a snapshot of the habit you reach for first — including when someone says they were hurt.

## Before you start

- [ ] Ask what this conversation is for: **a decision, a listen, a plan, or ideas.** Then match that for a few minutes.
- [ ] If you tend to jump to a fix, set a timer in your head and just listen for two minutes.
- [ ] If you tend to keep the peace, plan to state your own view in the first half of the conversation, not only at the end.

## If someone says they were hurt

The quiz asks what you would do first in that moment. All four first moves are reasonable. Use this as a menu, not as a score:

- [ ] **Clarity first:** Ask what they need from you now: an apology, an explanation, or a change.
- [ ] **Connection first:** Let them talk it all out first, even if you remember it differently. Reflect back one thing you heard before you solve it: “I can see why that felt unfair.”
- [ ] **Structure first:** Look back for the exact line that landed wrong. Specific examples help more than general feelings.
- [ ] **Spark first:** Apologize warmly, then help you both get back to feeling normal.

## Watch-outs under stress

These are the “watch out” lines from the public result texts, not a diagnosis:

- [ ] **Pathfinder:** You may get more blunt and skip the “why” — others can hear that as impatience. Add one sentence of context or appreciation, and ask one question about how they see it, before you answer.
- [ ] **Bridge-Builder:** You may go along with others to keep the peace and keep your real view to yourself. A kind but clear “no” is allowed: “I can't take this on this week.”
- [ ] **Blueprint:** You may go quiet to think, and others can read the silence as disagreement. It is fine to say “Let me come back to you on that by 3 pm.”
- [ ] **Spark:** You may talk faster and jump between ideas, and the main point can get lost. Cut the draft (or the monologue) and keep the single key point.
- [ ] **Navigator:** You may lose your range and fall back on one style — notice which one. That is often your real default.

## Close the loop

- [ ] After listening, summarize the decision or next step so the warmth turns into action.
- [ ] If you need time to think, say when you will come back: “Let me come back to you on that by 3 pm.”
- [ ] If the next step is an idea, write down one owner and one date before you walk away.
- [ ] If two of you keep missing each other, say so in situations, not scores: “When plans change last minute, I want a new list; you want to check how everyone feels.”

## Limits (please read)

This list, and the quiz it comes from, are for adults (18+), for fun and self-reflection only. They are not a psychological, clinical or diagnostic tool, and they have not been scientifically validated. They must not be used to predict whether a relationship will work, or to make hiring or career decisions. If you are struggling, a quiz is not a substitute for talking to a qualified professional.
